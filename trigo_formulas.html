A) Trigonometric Formulae Summary

Square of Trigonometric Ratios

\[ \begin{aligned} \sin^2 A + \cos^2 A &= 1 \\ 1 + \tan^2 A &= \sec^2 A \\ 1 + \cot^2 A &= \csc^2 A \end{aligned} \]

Compound Angle Formulae

\[ \begin{aligned} \sin(A \pm B) &= \sin A \cos B \pm \cos A \sin B \\ \cos(A \pm B) &= \cos A \cos B \mp \sin A \sin B \\ \tan(A \pm B) &= \frac{\tan A \pm \tan B}{1 \mp \tan A \tan B} \end{aligned} \]

Double Angle Formulae

\[ \begin{aligned} \sin 2A &= 2 \sin A \cos A \\ \cos 2A &= \cos^2 A - \sin^2 A \\ &= 1 - 2\sin^2 A \\ &= 2\cos^2 A - 1 \\ \tan 2A &= \frac{2\tan A}{1 - \tan^2 A} \\ \sin^2 A &= \frac{1 - \cos 2A}{2} \\ \cos^2 A &= \frac{1 + \cos 2A}{2} \end{aligned} \]

Product-to-sum Formulae

\[ \begin{aligned} \sin A \cos B &= \sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) + \sin\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) \\ \cos A \cos B &= \cos\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) + \cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) \\ \sin A \sin B &= -\cos\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) + \cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) \end{aligned} \]

Sum-to-product Formulae

\[ \begin{aligned} \sin A + \sin B &= 2 \sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) \\ \sin A - \sin B &= 2 \cos\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \sin\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) \\ \cos A + \cos B &= 2 \cos\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) \\ \cos A - \cos B &= -2 \sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right) \sin\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right) \end{aligned} \]

Type 1: Reciprocals and Squares of Trigonometric Ratios

Example 1

Prove the following identities.

(a) \(\frac{\tan^4 x - 1}{1 - 2\cos^2 x} = \sec^4 x\)

(b) \(\frac{\csc A + \csc B}{\cot A + \cot B} = \frac{\cot A - \cot B}{\csc A - \csc B}\)

(a)

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\tan^4 x - 1}{1 - 2\cos^2 x} &= \frac{(\tan^2 x + 1)(\tan^2 x - 1)}{1 - 2\cos^2 x} \\ &= \frac{\sec^2 x \left( \frac{\sin^2 x}{\cos^2 x} - 1 \right)}{1 - 2\cos^2 x} \\ &= \frac{\frac{1}{\cos^2 x} \left( \frac{\sin^2 x - \cos^2 x}{\cos^2 x} \right)}{\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x - 2\cos^2 x} \\ &= \frac{\sec^4 x (\sin^2 x - \cos^2 x)}{\sin^2 x - \cos^2 x} \\ &= \sec^4 x \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key: Where there are a lot of squares in the question, you can make use of either the square identities or sum of trigonometric square identities.

(b)

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\csc A + \csc B}{\cot A + \cot B} \times \frac{\cot A - \cot B}{\cot A - \cot B} &= \frac{(\csc A + \csc B)(\cot A - \cot B)}{\cot^2 A - \cot^2 B} \\ &= \frac{(\csc A + \csc B)(\cot A - \cot B)}{(\csc^2 A - 1) - (\csc^2 B - 1)} \\ &= \frac{(\csc A + \csc B)(\cot A - \cot B)}{\csc^2 A - \csc^2 B} \\ &= \frac{(\csc A + \csc B)(\cot A - \cot B)}{(\csc A + \csc B)(\csc A - \csc B)} \\ &= \frac{\cot A - \cot B}{\csc A - \csc B} \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Method 1: Multiply conjugate

Alternative

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\csc A + \csc B}{\cot A + \cot B} \times \frac{\csc A - \csc B}{\csc A - \csc B} &= \frac{\csc^2 A - \csc^2 B}{(\cot A + \cot B)(\csc A - \csc B)} \\ &= \frac{(1 + \cot^2 A) - (1 + \cot^2 B)}{(\cot A + \cot B)(\csc A - \csc B)} \\ &= \frac{\cot^2 A - \cot^2 B}{(\cot A + \cot B)(\csc A - \csc B)} \\ &= \frac{(\cot A + \cot B)(\cot A - \cot B)}{(\cot A + \cot B)(\csc A - \csc B)} \\ &= \frac{\cot A - \cot B}{\csc A - \csc B} \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Method 2: Multiply opposite denominator

Type 2: Compound Angle Formulae

Example 3

Solve the following equations for \(0 \le x \le \pi\).

(a) \(\tan 6x - \tan 2x - \tan 6x \tan 2x = 1\)

(b) \(\frac{1 + \tan x}{1 - \tan x} = \cot(x + \frac{\pi}{2})\)

(a)

\[ \begin{aligned} \tan 6x - \tan 2x - \tan 6x \tan 2x &= 1 \\ \tan 6x - \tan 2x &= 1 + \tan 6x \tan 2x \\ \frac{\tan 6x - \tan 2x}{1 + \tan 6x \tan 2x} &= 1 \\ \tan(6x - 2x) &= 1 \\ \tan 4x &= 1 \\ 4x &= \frac{\pi}{4} \text{ or } \frac{5\pi}{4} \text{ or } \frac{9\pi}{4} \text{ or } \frac{13\pi}{4} \\ x &= \frac{\pi}{16} \text{ or } \frac{5\pi}{16} \text{ or } \frac{9\pi}{16} \text{ or } \frac{13\pi}{16} \end{aligned} \]

(b)

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{1 + \tan x}{1 - \tan x} &= \cot(x + \frac{\pi}{2}) \\ \frac{\tan \frac{\pi}{4} + \tan x}{1 - \tan \frac{\pi}{4} \tan x} &= \cot(x + \frac{\pi}{2}) \\ \tan(x + \frac{\pi}{4}) &= \tan\left[ \frac{\pi}{2} - (x + \frac{\pi}{2}) \right] \text{ or } \tan(x + \frac{\pi}{4}) = \tan\left[ \frac{3\pi}{2} - (x + \frac{\pi}{2}) \right] \\ x + \frac{\pi}{4} &= x \text{ (rej.) or } x + \frac{\pi}{4} = \pi - x \\ x &= \frac{3\pi}{8} \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key: \(\cot x = \tan(90^\circ - x)\)

Type 3: Double Angle Formulae

Example 4 [2013-DSE-M2-Q7]

(a) Prove the identity \(\tan x = \frac{\sin 2x}{1 + \cos 2x}\).

(b) Using (a), prove the identity \(\tan y = \frac{\sin 8y \cos 4y \cos 2y}{(1 + \cos 8y)(1 + \cos 4y)(1 + \cos 2y)}\).

(a)

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\sin 2x}{1 + \cos 2x} &= \frac{2\sin x \cos x}{1 + (2\cos^2 x - 1)} \\ &= \frac{2\sin x \cos x}{2\cos^2 x} \\ &= \tan x \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key: Try to eliminate the 1 when considering which form to use.

(b)

\[ \begin{aligned} &\frac{\sin 8y \cos 4y \cos 2y}{(1 + \cos 8y)(1 + \cos 4y)(1 + \cos 2y)} \\ &= \tan 4y \frac{\cos 4y \cos 2y}{(1 + \cos 4y)(1 + \cos 2y)} \\ &= \frac{\sin 4y}{1 + \cos 4y} \times \frac{\cos 2y}{1 + \cos 2y} \\ &= \tan 2y \times \frac{\cos 2y}{1 + \cos 2y} \\ &= \frac{\sin 2y}{1 + \cos 2y} \\ &= \tan y \end{aligned} \]

More Examples

Example 2

It is given that \(\csc A + \cot A = k\).

(a) Express \(\csc A - \cot A\) in terms of \(k\).

(b) If \(\csc A\) and \(\cot A\) are the distinct roots of the quadratic equation \(9x^2 - 27x + p = 0\), where \(p\) is a constant, find the values of \(k\) and \(p\).

(a)

\[ \begin{aligned} \csc^2 A - \cot^2 A &= 1 \\ (\csc A + \cot A)(\csc A - \cot A) &= 1 \\ \csc A - \cot A &= \frac{1}{k} \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key: Instead of squaring both sides, the conjugates have a difference of reciprocals.

(b)

\[ \begin{aligned} \csc A + \cot A &= k = \frac{27}{9} \\ k &= 3 \\ \begin{cases} \csc A + \cot A = 3 \\ \csc A - \cot A = \frac{1}{3} \end{cases} \\ \text{Solving, } \csc A &= \frac{5}{3}, \cot A = \frac{4}{3} \\ \frac{p}{9} &= \csc A \cot A = \frac{20}{9} \\ p &= 20 \end{aligned} \]

Example 5 [2022-DSE-M2-Q2]

(a) Let \(\frac{\pi}{4} < \theta < \frac{\pi}{2}\). Prove the identity \(\frac{\tan \theta}{1 - \cot \theta} + \frac{\cot \theta}{1 - \tan \theta} = 1 + \sec \theta \csc \theta\).

(b) Solve the equation \(\frac{\tan \theta}{1 - \cot \theta} + \frac{\cot \theta}{1 - \tan \theta} = 5\).

(a)

\[ \begin{aligned} &\frac{\tan \theta}{1 - \cot \theta} + \frac{\cot \theta}{1 - \tan \theta} \\ &= \frac{\tan^2 \theta}{\tan \theta - 1} + \frac{1}{\tan \theta - \tan^2 \theta} \\ &= \frac{\tan^2 \theta}{\tan \theta - 1} - \frac{1}{\tan \theta(\tan \theta - 1)} \\ &= \frac{\tan^3 \theta - 1}{\tan \theta(\tan \theta - 1)} \\ &= \frac{(\tan \theta - 1)(\tan^2 \theta + \tan \theta + 1)}{\tan \theta(\tan \theta - 1)} \\ &= \frac{\sec^2 \theta + \tan \theta}{\tan \theta} \\ &= 1 + \frac{\sec^2 \theta}{\tan \theta} \\ &= 1 + \frac{\sec^2 \theta \cos \theta}{\sin \theta} \\ &= 1 + \sec \theta \csc \theta \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key: When simplifying reciprocals, we can multiply on both numerator and denominator.

(b)

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{\tan \theta}{1 - \cot \theta} + \frac{\cot \theta}{1 - \tan \theta} &= 5 \\ 1 + \sec \theta \csc \theta &= 5 \\ \frac{1}{\cos \theta \sin \theta} &= 4 \\ \frac{2}{2 \cos \theta \sin \theta} &= 4 \\ \frac{2}{\sin 2\theta} &= 4 \\ \sin 2\theta &= \frac{1}{2} \\ 2\theta &= \pi - \frac{\pi}{6} \\ \theta &= \frac{5\pi}{12} \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key: When there is \(\cos x \sin x\) usually it is double angle formula.

It is given that \(\tan \theta + \sec \theta = \frac{2}{5}\). By considering \(\tan \theta - \sec \theta\), find \(\tan \theta\) and \(\csc \theta\).

\[ \begin{aligned} 1 + \tan^2 \theta &= \sec^2 \theta \\ (\sec \theta + \tan \theta)(\sec \theta - \tan \theta) &= 1 \\ \frac{2}{5}(\sec \theta - \tan \theta) &= 1 \\ \sec \theta - \tan \theta &= \frac{5}{2} \end{aligned} \]
\[ \begin{cases} \tan \theta + \sec \theta = \frac{2}{5} \\ \tan \theta - \sec \theta = -\frac{5}{2} \end{cases} \]
\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Solving, } \tan \theta &= -\frac{21}{20}, \sec \theta = \frac{29}{20} \\ \tan \theta < 0 \text{ and } \sec \theta > 0 &\text{ implies } \csc \theta < 0. \\ \csc \theta &= -\frac{29}{21} \end{aligned} \]

B) Sequences related to Trigonometric Ratios

Example 1 [Textbook P.10.75 – Revision Exercise 10 – Q44]

Find the value of \(\tan 190^\circ \tan 280^\circ + \tan 200^\circ \tan 290^\circ + \tan 210^\circ \tan 300^\circ + \ldots + \tan 260^\circ \tan 350^\circ\).

\[ \begin{aligned} &\tan 190^\circ \tan 280^\circ + \tan 200^\circ \tan 290^\circ + \tan 210^\circ \tan 300^\circ + \ldots + \tan 260^\circ \tan 350^\circ \\ &= \tan 10^\circ \left(-\frac{1}{\tan 10^\circ}\right) + \tan 20^\circ \left(-\frac{1}{\tan 20^\circ}\right) + \ldots + \tan 80^\circ \left(-\frac{1}{\tan 80^\circ}\right) \\ &= (-1) \times 8 \\ &= -8 \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key: Convert the angles to \(0^\circ\) to \(90^\circ\) for ease of calculation.

Example 2 [Textbook P.10.75 – Revision Exercise 10 – Q45]

Find the value of \(\sin^2 91^\circ + \sin^2 93^\circ + \sin^2 95^\circ + \ldots + \sin^2 179^\circ\).

\[ \begin{aligned} &\sin^2 91^\circ + \sin^2 93^\circ + \sin^2 95^\circ + \ldots + \sin^2 135^\circ + \ldots + \sin^2 177^\circ + \sin^2 179^\circ \\ &= \sin^2 89^\circ + \sin^2 87^\circ + \sin^2 85^\circ + \ldots + \sin^2 45^\circ + \ldots + \sin^2 3^\circ + \sin^2 1^\circ \\ &= (\sin^2 1^\circ + \sin^2 89^\circ) + (\sin^2 3^\circ + \sin^2 87^\circ) + \ldots + (\sin^2 43^\circ + \sin^2 47^\circ) + \sin^2 45^\circ \\ &= (\cos^2 1^\circ + \sin^2 1^\circ) + (\cos^2 3^\circ + \sin^2 3^\circ) + \ldots + (\cos^2 43^\circ + \sin^2 43^\circ) + \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 \\ &= 1 \times 22 + \frac{1}{2} \\ &= 22.5 \end{aligned} \]

Remarks: Sum = Numbers of terms \(45 \times \frac{1}{2} = 22.5\)

C) Trigonometric Equations - Type 1: Range Adjustment

This skill is applied when the unknown involves other manipulations (\(+ - \times \div\) other constants).

Example 1

Solve \(\sin 2x = \frac{1}{2}\) for \(0^\circ \leq x \leq 360^\circ\)

\[ \begin{aligned} 0 \leq x \leq 360^\circ &\implies 0 \leq 2x \leq 720^\circ \\ \sin 2x &= \frac{1}{2} \\ 2x &= 30^\circ \text{ or } 150^\circ \text{ or } 390^\circ \text{ or } 510^\circ \\ x &= 15^\circ \text{ or } 75^\circ \text{ or } 195^\circ \text{ or } 255^\circ \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key 1: Change the range base on the size of the angle. (Rough work)

🔑 Key 2: Start a new cycle by adding \(360^\circ\) to the original angle, which is equivalent to adding \(180^\circ\) to the final angle.

Example 2 [2025-DSE-Math-II-Q39]

For \(90^\circ < \theta < 270^\circ\), how many roots does the equation \(\tan^3 \theta = 2 \tan \theta\) have?

A. 2
B. 3
C. 4
D. 5

\[ \begin{aligned} \tan^3 \theta &= 2 \tan \theta \\ \tan \theta (\tan^2 \theta - 2) &= 0 \\ \tan \theta = 0 \text{ or } \tan \theta = \sqrt{2} &\text{ or } \tan \theta = -\sqrt{2} \end{aligned} \]

For \(90^\circ < \theta < 270^\circ\), Quadrant II is negative and Quadrant III is positive.
Therefore, there are 3 roots in total.

Type 2: Unifying same trigonometric ratio

Example 3

Solve \(3\sin^2 x - \cos x \sin x - 1 = 0\) for \(0^\circ \leq x \leq 360^\circ\)

\[ \begin{aligned} 3\sin^2 x - \cos x \sin x - 1 &= 0 \\ 3\sin^2 x - \cos x \sin x - (\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x) &= 0 \\ 2\sin^2 x - \cos x \sin x - \cos^2 x &= 0 \\ 2\tan^2 x - \tan x - 1 &= 0 \\ (2\tan x + 1)(\tan x - 1) &= 0 \\ \tan x = -\frac{1}{2} &\text{ or } \tan x = 1 \\ x = 45^\circ \text{ or } 225^\circ &\text{ or } 153^\circ \text{ or } 333^\circ \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key 1: The constant term can be changed to multiple of \(\sin^2 x + \cos^2 x\).

🔑 Key 2: Divide the whole expression by \(\cos^2 x\).

Example 4

Solve \(\cos x = -\sin 40^\circ\) for \(0^\circ \leq x \leq 360^\circ\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \cos x &= -\sin 40^\circ \\ \cos x &= \cos(90^\circ + 40^\circ) \text{ or } \cos x = \cos(270^\circ - 40^\circ) \\ x &= 130^\circ \text{ or } x = 230^\circ \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key: Let \(\theta = 40^\circ\), \(\cos(90^\circ + \theta) = \cos(270^\circ - \theta) = -\sin \theta\).

Example 5

Solve \(\sin x + \sqrt{3}\cos x - 2 = 0\) for \(0^\circ \leq x \leq 360^\circ\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \sin x + \sqrt{3}\cos x - 2 &= 0 \\ \sqrt{3}\cos x &= 2 - \sin x \\ 3\cos^2 x &= 4 - 4\sin x + \sin^2 x \\ 3 - 3\sin^2 x &= 4 - 4\sin x + \sin^2 x \\ 4\sin^2 x - 4\sin x + 1 &= 0 \\ (2\sin x - 1)^2 &= 0 \\ \sin x &= \frac{1}{2} \text{ (repeated)} \\ x &= 30^\circ \text{ or } x = 150^\circ \text{ (rej.)} \end{aligned} \]
🔑 Key: When the equation involves squaring both sides, you need check for the validity of the roots.

(Hint: You need to use the quadratic formula first)

Example 6

\(\cos \theta\) and \(\frac{1}{\sin \theta}\) are the roots of the quadratic equation \(8x^2 + kx + 15 = 0\), where \(k\) is a constant and \(180^\circ < \theta < 270^\circ\).

(a) Find the value of \(\tan \theta\).
(b) Find the values of \(\sin \theta\) and \(\cos \theta\).
(c) Find the value of \(k\).

(a)

\[ \begin{aligned} \cos \theta \left(\frac{1}{\sin \theta}\right) &= \frac{15}{8} \\ \tan \theta &= \frac{8}{15} \end{aligned} \]

(b)

A) Maximum and Minimum values of Trigonometric Functions (Part 1)

By using the inequalities \(-1 \le \sin \theta \le 1\) and \(-1 \le \cos \theta \le 1\), we can further investigate the maximum and minimum values of sine and cosine functions in different forms.

The following assumptions are held in this chapter:

  • Unless otherwise stated, the domain of all functions in this note refers to \(0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ\).
  • There is only one trigonometric function involved; in case there are more than one, convert them into the same function by using trigonometric identities.

Type 1: Linear Functions (\(y = a \sin \theta + b\) / \(y = a \cos \theta + b\))

Method: By substitution
Step 1 Let \(t = \sin \theta\) or \(t = \cos \theta\), note that \(-1 \le t \le 1\).
Step 2 It is transformed to a strictly increasing / decreasing linear function \(y = at + b\).
Step 3 Substitute \(t = 1\) and \(t = -1\) into the function and obtain the max/min values.

Examples:

1. Find the maximum/minimum value of \(y = 2 \sin \theta + 3\) where \(0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ\).

Consider the linear function \(y = 2t + 3\),

Maximum value: \(y = 2(1) + 3 = 5\)

Minimum value: \(y = 2(-1) + 3 = 1\)

Exercises:

1. [Level 1] Find the maximum/minimum value of these functions, where \(0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ\).

(a) \(y = 3 \sin \theta - 5\)

Consider the linear function \(y = 3t - 5\),

Maximum value: \(y = 2(1) - 5 = -3\)

Minimum value: \(y = 2(-1) - 5 = -7\)

(b) \(y = 3 - 4 \cos \theta\)

Consider the linear function \(y = 4t - 3\),

Maximum value: \(y = 3 - 4(-1) = 7\)

Minimum value: \(y = 3 - 4(1) = -1\)

Type 2: Quadratic Functions

(a) Basic form: \(y = a \sin^2 \theta + b\) / \(y = a \cos^2 \theta + b\)

Method: By substitution
Step 1 Let \(t = \sin \theta\) or \(t = \cos \theta\), note that \(-1 \le t \le 1\), which implies \(0 \le t^2 \le 1\).
Step 2 It is transformed to a quadratic function \(y = at^2 + b\).
Step 3 Substitute \(t^2 = 0\) and \(t^2 = 1\) into the function and obtain the max/min values.

Examples:

2. Find the maximum/minimum value of \(y = 2 \sin^2 \theta - 3\) where \(0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ\).

Consider the quadratic function \(y = 2t^2 - 3\),

Maximum value: \(y = 2(1) - 3 = -1\)

Minimum value: \(y = 2(0) - 3 = -3\)

Exercises:

2. [Level 1] Find the maximum/minimum value of these functions, where \(0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ\).

(a) \(y = 2 \cos^2 \theta - 5\)

Consider the quadratic function \(y = 3t^2 - 5\),

Maximum value: \(y = 2(0) - 5 = -3\)

Minimum value: \(y = 2(1) - 5 = -5\)

(b) \(y = 3 - 4 \sin^2 \theta\)

Consider the quadratic function \(y = 4t^2 - 3\),

Maximum value: \(y = 3 - 4(0) = 3\)

Minimum value: \(y = 3 - 4(1) = -1\)

(c) \(y = 1 - 2 \sin^2 \theta + 3 \cos^2 \theta\) (Hint: use trigonometric identities)

Note that \(y = 1 - 2 \sin^2 \theta + 3 \cos^2 \theta = 1 - 2 \sin^2 \theta + 3(1 - \sin^2 \theta) = 4 - 5 \sin^2 \theta\)

Consider the quadratic function \(y = 5t^2 - 4\),

Maximum value: \(y = 5 - 4(0) = 4\)

Minimum value: \(y = 5 - 4(1) = -1\)

(b) Perfect Square form: \(y = (a \sin \theta + b)^2\) / \(y = (a \cos \theta + b)^2\)

Method: By substitution
Step 1 Let \(t = \sin \theta\) or \(t = \cos \theta\), note that \(-1 \le t \le 1\).
Step 2 It is transformed to a quadratic function \(y = (at + b)^2\).
Step 3 Check: Substitute \(t = 1\) and \(-1\). If 0 is inside the range of \(at + b\) after substitution, the minimum value is 0. The maximum value is obtained when \(t = 1\) or \(-1\), whichever has a larger square.
Step 4 Check: Substitute \(t = 1\) and \(-1\). If 0 is NOT inside the range of \(at + b\) after substitution, the minimum value and maximum value are obtained when \(t = 1\) and \(-1\).

Examples:

3. Find the maximum/minimum value of these functions, where \(0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ\).

(a) \(y = (2 \sin \theta - 5)^2\)

Consider the quadratic function \(y = (2t - 5)^2\),

By substituting \(t = 1\) and \(-1\) into \(1 - 2t\), the values are \(-1\) and \(3\) respectively, where \(0\) is inside the range.

Minimum value: \(y = 0^2 = 0\)

Minimum value: \(y = 0^2 = 0\)

(b) \(y = -(4 \cos \theta - 3)^2\)

Consider the quadratic function \(y = -(4t - 3)^2\),

By substituting \(t = 1\) and \(-1\) into \(4t - 3\), the values are \(1\) and \(-7\) respectively, where \(0\) is inside the range.

Maximum value (as printed in key): \(y = (4 \times (-1) - 3)^2 = (-7)^2 = 49\) (Note: accounting for the negative sign in \(-(4t - 3)^2\), max = 0 and min = -49)

Minimum value (as printed in key): \(y = (4 \times 1 - 3)^2 = 1^2 = 1\)

(c) \(y = 4 \sin^2 \theta + 4 \sin \theta + 1\) (Hint: Is it a perfect square?)

Note that \(y = 4 \sin^2 \theta + 4 \sin \theta + 2 = (2 \sin \theta + 1)^2\)

Consider the quadratic function \(y = (2t + 1)^2\),

By substituting \(t = 1\) and \(-1\) into \(2t + 1\), the values are \(3\) and \(-1\) respectively (key writes \(3\) and \(1\), noting \(0\) is inside the range).

Maximum value: \(y = 3^2 = 9\)

Minimum value: \(y = 1^2 = 1\) (or \(0\) at \(t = -\frac{1}{2}\))

(c) Quadratic form: \(y = a \sin^2 \theta + b \sin \theta + c\) / \(a \cos^2 \theta + b \cos \theta + c\)

Method: By completing square
Step 1 Let \(t = \sin \theta\) or \(t = \cos \theta\), note that \(-1 \le t \le 1\).
Step 2 It is transformed to a quadratic function \(y = at^2 + bt + c\).
Step 3 Complete the square for the expression to the form \(y = a(t - h)^2 + k\).
Step 4 Check: If vertex's x-coordinate \(h\) is in the range of -1 to 1, substitute \(t = 1\), \(-1\) and \(h\) to the expression, then assign the maximum and minimum values. If vertex's x-coordinate \(h\) is NOT in the range of -1 to 1, substitute \(t = 1\) and \(-1\) ONLY to the expression, then assign the maximum and minimum values.

Note that \(y = (\sin \theta + 3)^2 - 1\)

By substituting \(t = 1\), \(-\frac{1}{2}\) and \(-1\) to \(-2\left(t + \frac{1}{2}\right)^2 + \frac{5}{2}\), the values are \(-2\), \(\frac{5}{2}\) and \(2\) respectively.

A) Maximum and Minimum values of Trigonometric Functions (Part 2)

Type 3: Rational Functions (\(y = \frac{a \sin \theta + b}{c \sin \theta + d}\) / \(y = \frac{a \cos \theta + b}{c \cos \theta + d}\))

Method: By long division

Step 1 Let \(t = \sin \theta\) or \(t = \cos \theta\), note that \(-1 \le t \le 1\).
Step 2 Perform long division of numerator by the denominator to separate the function into forms of \(m + \frac{n}{c t + d}\).
Step 3 Find the value of \(t\) which sets the denominator \(c t + d = 0\) (i.e. \(t = -\frac{d}{c}\)).
Step 4 Check: If \(-\frac{d}{c}\) is in the range of -1 to 1, the denominator can be 0, so there is no maximum and minimum. If \(-\frac{d}{c}\) is NOT in the range of -1 to 1, substitute \(t = 1\) and \(-1\) ONLY to the expression, then assign the maximum and minimum values.

Examples:

5. Find the maximum/minimum value of these functions, where \(0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ\).

(a) \(y = \frac{2 - \sin \theta}{2 + \sin \theta}\)

By long division \(|2 - \sin \theta| \div |2 + \sin \theta|\) gives quotient -1 and remainder 4, or by observing coefficients:

\(2 - \sin \theta = -|2 + \sin \theta| + 4\).

Hence, we have \(y = \frac{-1|2 + \sin \theta| + 4}{2 + \sin \theta} = -1 + \frac{4}{2 + \sin \theta}\).

Consider the function \(y = -1 + \frac{4}{2+t}\), whose denominator cannot be 0 for the range of \(-1 \le t \le 1\).

By substituting \(t = 1\) and \(-1\) to \(-1 + \frac{4}{2+t}\), the values are \(\frac{1}{3}\) and 3 respectively.

Maximum value = \(-1 + \frac{4}{2+|-1|} = 3\), Minimum value = \(-1 + \frac{4}{2+1} = \frac{1}{3}\)

(b) \(y = \frac{4\cos\theta}{1 - 2\cos\theta}\)

By long division \(|4\cos \theta| \div |1 - 2\cos \theta|\) gives quotient -2 and remainder 2, or by observing coefficients:

\[ 4 \cos \theta = - 2 (1 - 2 \cos \theta) + 2. \]

Hence, we have \(y = -2 + \frac{2}{1 - 2\cos\theta}\).

Consider the function \(y = -2 + \frac{2}{1 - 2t}\). When \(t = \frac{1}{2}\), denominator can be 0.

Hence, there is no maximum nor minimum.

(c) \(y = \frac{7 - 3\sin^2\theta}{2 - \sin^2\theta}\)

By long division \(|7 - 3\sin^2\theta| \div |2 - \sin^2\theta|\) gives quotient 3 and remainder 1, or by observing coefficients:

\[ 7 - 3 \sin^{2} \theta = 3 (2 - \sin^{2} \theta) + 1. \]

Hence, we have \(y = 3 + \frac{1}{2 - \sin^2\theta}\).

Consider the function \(y = 3 + \frac{1}{2 - t^2}\), where the denominator cannot be 0 for the range of \(0 \leq t^2 \leq 1\).

Maximum value \(= 3 + \frac{1}{2 - 1} = 4\), Minimum value \(= 3 + \frac{1}{2 - 0} = \frac{7}{2}\)

Exercises:

5. [Level 2] Find the maximum/minimum value of these functions, where \(0^\circ \leq \theta \leq 360^\circ\).

(a) \(y = -\frac{6\sin\theta}{3\sin\theta - 1}\)

By long division \(|-6\sin \theta |\div |3\sin \theta -1|\) gives quotient -2 and remainder -2, or by observing coefficients:

\[ y = \frac{- 2 ( 3 \sin \theta - 1 ) - 2}{3 \sin \theta - 1} = - 2 - \frac{2}{3 \sin \theta - 1}. \]

Consider the function \(y = -2 - \frac{2}{3t - 1}\). \(t = \frac{1}{3}\) is in the range of \(-1 \leq t \leq 1\), which means there is no maximum nor minimum.

(b) \(y = \frac{4\cos\theta}{2 - \cos\theta}\)

By long division \(|4\cos \theta| \div |2 - \cos \theta|\) gives quotient -4 and remainder 8, or by observing coefficients:

\[ 4 \cos \theta = - 4 (2 - \cos \theta) + 8. \]

Hence, we have \(y = -4 + \frac{8}{2 - \cos\theta}\).

Consider the function \(y = -4 + \frac{8}{2 - t}\), whose denominator cannot be 0 for the range of \(-1 \leq t \leq 1\).

Maximum value \(= -4 + \frac{8}{2 - 1} = 4\), Minimum value \(= -4 + \frac{8}{2 - (-1)} = -\frac{4}{3}\)

(c) \(y = \frac{2 \cos^2 \theta}{2 + \sin^2 \theta}\)

By long division \((-2 \sin^2 \theta + 2) \div (\sin^2 \theta + 2)\) gives quotient \(-2\) and remainder \(6\), or by observing coefficients:

\[ 2 - 2 \sin^2 \theta = -2 (\sin^2 \theta + 2) + 6. \]

Hence, we have \(y = -2 + \frac{6}{2 + \sin^2 \theta}\).

Maximum value \(= -2 + \frac{6}{2+0} = 1\), Minimum value \(= -2 + \frac{6}{2+1} = 0\)

Type 4: Restricted Specific Range

On few occasions, the question restricts the range of the trigonometric function such that it is not a must to substitute 1 and -1 all the time, particularly for CE Exam Questions.

Examples:

6. [Modified-2012PP-DSE-MATH-II-Q23] Find the largest and least value of \(\frac{30}{3 \sin^2 \theta + 2 \sin^2 (90^\circ - \theta)}\) for \(135^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ\).

Note that \(\frac{30}{3 \sin^2 \theta + 2 \sin^3 (90^\circ - \theta)} = \frac{30}{3 \sin^2 \theta + 2 \cos^2 \theta} = \frac{30}{\sin^2 \theta + 2}\)

For \(135^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ\), Minimum value of \(\sin \theta = 0\) (When \(\theta = 180^\circ\)), Maximum value of \(\sin \theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\) (When \(\theta = 135^\circ\))

Maximum value \(= \frac{30}{0^2 + 2} = 15\), Minimum value \(= \frac{30}{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 + 2} = 12\)

Exercises:

6. [Level 2] Find the maximum/minimum value of these functions.

(a) \(y = 3 - 4 \cos \theta\) where \(90^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ\) (From Exercise 1)

For \(90^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ, -1 \le \cos \theta \le 0\).

Maximum value = \(3 - 4(-1) = 7\), Minimum value = \(3 - 4(0) = 3\).

(b) \(y = (1 - 4 \sin \theta)^2\) where \(90^\circ \le \theta \le 150^\circ\) (From Exercise 3)

(c) \(y = 2 \sin^2 \theta - 2 \sin \theta - 4\) where \(90^\circ \le \theta \le 150^\circ\) (From Exercise 4)

7. [Level 2] Find the maximum/minimum value of these functions for \(0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ\).

(a) \(y = 5 - 3 \cos \theta\)

Maximum value: \(5 - 3(-1) = 8\), Minimum value: \(5 - 3(1) = 2\)

(b) \(y = \frac{48}{3 \cos^2 \theta + 4 \sin^2 \theta}\)

Note that \(y = \frac{48}{3 \cos^2 \theta + 4\sin^2 \theta} = \frac{48}{3 \cos^2 \theta + 3\sin^2 \theta + \sin^2 \theta} = \frac{48}{3 + \sin^2 \theta}\)

Maximum value: \(\frac{48}{3 + |\theta|} = 16\), Minimum value: \(\frac{48}{3 + |1|} = 12\)

(c) \(y = \cos^2 \theta - 2 \cos \theta + 2\)

Note that \(y = |\cos \theta - 1|^2 + 1\),

Maximum value: \((-2)^2 + 1 = 5\), Minimum value: \(0^2 + 1 = 1\)

(d) \(y = \frac{6+6 \sin \theta}{3+2 \sin \theta}\) (2526-WYK-S4-Final-II-Q42)

Note that \(y = \frac{6+6 \sin \theta}{3+2 \sin \theta} = \frac{3|3+2 \sin \theta| - 3}{3+2 \sin \theta} = 3 - \frac{3}{3+2 \sin \theta}\)

Maximum value: \(3 - \frac{3}{3+2|1|} = \frac{12}{5}\), Minimum value: \(3 - \frac{3}{3+2|-1|} = 0\)

(e) \(y = |\sin \theta + 1|^2 |\sin \theta - 1|^2\)

Note that \(y = |\sin^2 \theta - 1|^2 = \cos^4 \theta\),

Maximum value: \(1^4 = 1\), Minimum value: \(0^4 = 0\)

(f) \(y = \frac{2|\sin \theta - 2|^2}{\sin^2 \theta - 4 \sin \theta + 3}\) (Challenging)

Note that \(y = \frac{2|\sin \theta - 2|^2}{\sin^3 \theta - 4 \sin \theta + 3} = \frac{2|\sin \theta - 2|^2}{|\sin \theta - 2|^2 - 1} = \frac{2[|\sin \theta - 2|^2 - 1] + 2}{|\sin \theta - 2|^2 - 1} = 2 + \frac{2}{|\sin \theta - 2|^2 - 1}\)

Since when \(\sin \theta = 1\), \(|\sin \theta - 2|^2 - 1 = 0\), there is no maximum value.

Minimum value: \(2 + \frac{2}{|-1 - 2|^2 - 1} = \frac{9}{4}\)

(g) \(y = \frac{4\sin^2\theta - 4\sin\theta - 7}{|2\sin\theta - 1|^2}\) (Challenging) (2425-WYK-S4-Final-II-Q44)

Note that \(y = \frac{4\sin^2\theta - 4\sin\theta - 7}{|2\sin\theta - 1|^2} = \frac{[2\sin\theta - 1]^2 - 8}{[2\sin\theta - 1]^2} = 1 - \frac{8}{[2\sin\theta - 1]^2}\)

Since when \(\sin \theta = \frac{1}{2}\), \(|2\sin \theta - 1|^2 = 0\), there is no minimum value.

Maximum value: \(1 - \frac{8}{\left[2(-1) - 1\right]^2} = \frac{1}{9}\)

(h) \(y = -\tan^2\theta - 2\tan \theta\)

Note that \(y = -\tan^2\theta - 2\tan \theta = -\left|\tan^2\theta + 2\tan \theta + 1\right| + 1 = -\left|\tan \theta + 1\right|^2 + 1\)

Maximum value: \(-\left|0\right|^2 + 1 = 1\). No minimum value.
