Coordinate Geometry

Distance, slope, midpoint and section formulas.

Basics Lines

Basic Formulas

Two points A(a, m) and B(b, n)

Distance: √((a − b)² + (m − n)²) Slope: (m − n) / (a − b) Mid-point of AB: ((a + b)/2, (m + n)/2) Coordinates of the point which divided AB into ratio of u : v: ((av + bu)/(u + v), (mv + nu)/(u + v))

Slopes of Lines

Four cases of slope

  • Case 1: if (m − n)/(a − b) > 0, it is upward sloping.
  • Case 2: if (m − n)/(a − b) < 0, it is downward sloping.
  • Case 3: if m = n (y-coordinate equal), (m − n)/(a − b) = 0 and it is a horizontal line.
  • Case 4: if a = b (x-coordinate equal), (m − n)/(a − b) is undefined and it is a vertical line.

Two lines with slopes p and q

  • Case 1: if p = q, the two lines are parallel to each other.
  • Case 2: if p × q = −1, the two lines are perpendicular to each other.

The previous cases are not including situations when at least one of the lines are vertical lines.
