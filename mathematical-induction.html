Example 1 — [Modified-2026-DSE-M2-Q2]

(a) Using mathematical induction, prove that \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} = \frac{2n}{n+1}\) for all positive integers \(n\).

(Hint: \(1+2+3+\ldots+k = \frac{(1+k)k}{2}\).)

(b) Using (a), evaluate \(\displaystyle\frac{1}{2+4+6+\ldots+100} + \frac{1}{2+4+6+\ldots+102} + \frac{1}{2+4+6+\ldots+104} + \ldots + \frac{1}{2+4+6+\ldots+200}\).

Approach 1: Textbook Approach

(a) Let \(\mathrm{P}(n)\) be \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} = \frac{2n}{n+1}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{For } n=1,\quad & \\ \text{L.H.S.} &= \frac{1}{1} = 1 \\ \text{R.H.S.} &= \frac{2(1)}{1+1} = 1 = \text{L.H.S.} \end{aligned} \]

\(\mathrm{P}(1)\) is true.

Assume \(\mathrm{P}(m)\) is true for some positive integer \(m\)

\[ \text{i.e. } \sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} = \frac{2m}{m+1}. \]

When \(n = m+1\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{L.H.S.} &= \sum_{k=1}^{m+1}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} \\ &= \sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} + \frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+m+1} \\ &= \frac{2m}{m+1} + \frac{1}{\frac{[1+(m+1)](m+1)}{2}} \\ &= \frac{2m}{m+1} + \frac{2}{(m+2)(m+1)} \\ &= \frac{2m^2+4m+2}{(m+2)(m+1)} \\ &= \frac{2(m+1)^2}{(m+2)(m+1)} \\ &= \frac{2(m+1)}{m+1+1} \\ &= \text{R.H.S.} \end{aligned} \]

\(\mathrm{P}(m+1)\) is true.

By MI, \(\mathrm{P}(n)\) is true for all positive integer \(n\).

Notes:

  • 1: Using assumption
  • 1 f.t. for whole proof correct

Some notations you may use:

By MI, \(P(n)\) is true \(\forall n \in \mathbf{N}\)
(For all \(m\) belong to natural number)

Approach 2: Exam Marking Approach

Note that \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{1}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} = 1 = \frac{2(1)}{(1)+1}\).

The statement is true for \(n=1\).

Assume \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} = \frac{2m}{m+1}\) is true for some positive integer \(m\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{k=1}^{m+1}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} &= \sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} + \frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+m+1} \\ &= \frac{2m}{m+1} + \frac{1}{\frac{[1+(m+1)](m+1)}{2}} \\ &= \frac{2m}{m+1} + \frac{2}{(m+2)(m+1)} \\ &= \frac{2m^2+4m+2}{(m+2)(m+1)} \\ &= \frac{2(m+1)^2}{(m+2)(m+1)} \\ &= \frac{2(m+1)}{m+1+1} \\ &= \text{R.H.S.} \end{aligned} \]

The statement is true for \(n=m+1\) if it is true for \(n=m\).

By MI, the statement is true for all positive integer \(n\).

(b)

\[ \begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2+4+6+\ldots+100} + \frac{1}{2+4+6+\ldots+102} + \frac{1}{2+4+6+\ldots+104} + \ldots + \frac{1}{2+4+6+\ldots+200} \\ &= \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+50} + \frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+51} + \frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+52} + \ldots + \frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+100} \right) \\ &= \frac{1}{2}\left( \sum_{k=1}^{100}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} - \sum_{k=1}^{49}\frac{1}{1+2+3+\ldots+k} \right) \\ &= \frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{2 \times 100}{100+1} - \frac{2 \times 49}{49+1} \right) \\ &= \frac{51}{5050} \end{aligned} \]

Example 2 — Type 2: MI not starting with first term

Prove, by mathematical induction, that for all positive integers \(n\),

\(2 + (-1) + (-4) + \ldots + (2 - 3n) = \dfrac{(n+1)(4-3n)}{2}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Note that L.H.S.} &= 2 + (-1) = 1 \\ \text{R.H.S.} &= \frac{(1+1)(4-3(1))}{2} = 1 = \text{L.H.S.} \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, the statement is true for \(n=1\).

Assume that \(2 + (-1) + (-4) + \ldots + (2 - 3m) = \dfrac{(m+1)(4-3m)}{2}\), where \(m\) is a positive integer.

\[ \begin{aligned} &2 + (-1) + (-4) + \ldots + (2 - 3m) + (2 - 3(m+1)) \\ &= \frac{(m+1)(4-3m)}{2} + (2 - 3(m+1)) \\ &= \frac{(4m - 3m^2 + 4 - 3m) + 2(-3m - 1)}{2} \\ &= \frac{-3m^2 + m + 4 - 6m - 2}{2} \\ &= \frac{-3m^2 - 5m + 2}{2} \\ &= \frac{-(3m - 1)(m + 2)}{2} \\ &= \frac{((m+1)+1)(4 - 3(m+1))}{2} \end{aligned} \]

So, the statement is true for \(n = m+1\) if it is true for \(n = m\).

By mathematical induction, the statement is true for all positive integers \(n\).

Example 3 — Type 3: MI from \(n^{\text{th}}\) term to \(2n^{\text{th}}\) term

[2019-DSE-M2-Q7]

(a) Prove, by mathematical induction, that for all positive integers \(n\), \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=n}^{2n}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = \frac{n+1}{n(2n+1)}\).

(b) Using (a), evaluate \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=50}^{200}\frac{1}{k(k+1)}\).

(a)

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Note that L.H.S.} &= \sum_{k=1}^{2}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = \frac{1}{1(2)} + \frac{1}{2(3)} = \frac{2}{3}. \\ \text{R.H.S.} &= \frac{1+1}{1(2(1)+1)} = \frac{2}{3} = \text{L.H.S.} \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, the statement is true for \(n=1\).

Assume that \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=m}^{2m}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = \frac{m+1}{m(2m+1)}\), where \(m\) is a positive integer.

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{k=m+1}^{2(m+1)}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} &= \sum_{k=m}^{2m}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} - \frac{1}{m(m+1)} + \frac{1}{(2m+1)(2m+2)} + \frac{1}{(2m+2)(2m+3)} \\ &= \frac{m+1}{m(2m+1)} - \frac{1}{m(m+1)} + \frac{1}{(2m+1)(2m+2)} + \frac{1}{(2m+2)(2m+3)} \\ &= \left(\frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{2m+1}\right) - \left(\frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{m+1}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2m+1} - \frac{1}{2m+2}\right) + \left(\frac{1}{2m+2} - \frac{1}{2m+3}\right) \\ &= \frac{1}{m+1} - \frac{1}{2m+3} \\ &= \frac{m+2}{(m+1)(2m+3)} \\ &= \frac{(m+1)+1}{(m+1)(2(m+1)+1)} \end{aligned} \]

So, the statement is true for \(n = m+1\) if it is true for \(n = m\).

By mathematical induction, the statement is true for all positive integers \(n\).

(b)

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{k=50}^{200}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} &= \sum_{k=50}^{100}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} + \sum_{k=100}^{200}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} - \frac{1}{100(101)} \\ &= \frac{50+1}{50(2(50)+1)} + \frac{100+1}{100(2(100)+1)} - \frac{1}{10100} \\ &= \frac{151}{10050} \end{aligned} \]

Example 4 — Type 4: Deducing a new formula

[Level 1]

(a) Prove, by mathematical induction, that for all positive integers \(n\),

\(1 \times 2 \times 3 + 2 \times 3 \times 4 + 3 \times 4 \times 5 + \ldots + n(n+1)(n+2) = \dfrac{n(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}{4}\).

(b) Using (a) and the formula \(1 \times 2 + 2 \times 3 + 3 \times 4 + \ldots + n(n+1) = \dfrac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{3}\), prove that \(1^2 \times 2 + 2^2 \times 3 + 3^2 \times 4 + \ldots + n^2(n+1) = \dfrac{n(n+1)(n+2)(3n+1)}{12}\) for all positive integers.

(b)

Note that L.H.S. \(= 1 \times 2 \times 3 = 6\)

\[ \text{R.H.S.} = \frac{1(1+1)(1+2)(1+3)}{4} = 6 = \text{L.H.S.} \]

Therefore, the statement is true for \(n=1\).

Assume that \(1 \times 2 \times 3 + 2 \times 3 \times 4 + 3 \times 4 \times 5 + \ldots + m(m+1)(m+2) = \dfrac{m(m+1)(m+2)(m+3)}{4}\), where \(m\) is a positive integer.

\[ \begin{aligned} &1 \times 2 \times 3 + 2 \times 3 \times 4 + 3 \times 4 \times 5 + \ldots + m(m+1)(m+2) + (m+1)(m+1+1)(m+1+2) \\ &= \frac{m(m+1)(m+2)(m+3)}{4} + (m+1)(m+2)(m+3) \\ &= (m+1)(m+2)(m+3)\left[\frac{m}{4} + 1\right] \\ &= \frac{(m+1)(m+2)(m+3)(m+4)}{4} \\ &= \frac{(m+1)((m+1)+1)((m+1)+2)((m+1)+3)}{4} \end{aligned} \]

So, the statement is true for \(n = m+1\) if it is true for \(n = m\).

By mathematical induction, the statement is true for all positive integers \(n\).

(b) — Continued (Page 33)

\(1 \times 2 \times 3 + 2 \times 3 \times 4 + 3 \times 4 \times 5 + \ldots + n(n+1)(n+2) = \dfrac{n(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}{4}\)

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{m=1}^{n} m(m+1)(m+2) &= \frac{n(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}{4} \\ \sum_{m=1}^{n} m(m+1)(m+2) - \sum_{m=1}^{n} m(m+1)(2) &= \frac{n(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}{4} - \sum_{m=1}^{n} m(m+1)(2) \\ \sum_{m=1}^{n} m^2(m+1) &= \frac{n(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}{4} - 2\left[\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{3}\right] \\ \sum_{m=1}^{n} m^2(m+1) &= n(n+1)(n+2)\left(\frac{n+3}{4} - \frac{2}{3}\right) \\ \sum_{m=1}^{n} m^2(m+1) &= n(n+1)(n+2)\left(\frac{3n+9-8}{12}\right) \\ 1^2 \times 2 + 2^2 \times 3 + 3^2 \times 4 + \ldots + n^2(n+1) &= \frac{n(n+1)(n+2)(3n+1)}{12} \end{aligned} \]

Example 5 — Type 5: MI with recursive sequences

It is given that \(a_1, a_2, a_3, \ldots, a_n, \ldots\) is a sequence of numbers, where \(a_1 = 2\) and \(a_{r+1} = 3a_r - 1\) for \(r = 1, 2, 3, \ldots\). Prove, by mathematical induction, that for all positive integers \(n\), \(a_n = \dfrac{3^n + 1}{2}\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Note that L.H.S.} &= a_1 = 2 \\ \text{R.H.S.} &= \frac{3^1 + 1}{2} = 2 = \text{L.H.S.} \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, the statement is true for \(n=1\).

Assume that \(a_m = \dfrac{3^m + 1}{2}\), where \(m\) is a positive integer.

\[ \begin{aligned} a_{m+1} &= 3a_m - 1 \\ &= 3\left(\frac{3^m + 1}{2}\right) - 1 \\ &= \frac{3^{m+1} + 3 - 2}{2} \\ &= \frac{3^{m+1} + 1}{2} \end{aligned} \]

So, the statement is true for \(n = m+1\) if it is true for \(n = m\).

By mathematical induction, the statement is true for all positive integers \(n\).

Exercise 24 — [Level 1]

A sequence of numbers \(c_1, c_2, \ldots, c_n, \ldots\) is defined as \(c_1 = 2\) and \(c_{k+1} = 3c_k + 2k - 1\) for \(k = 1, 2, 3, \ldots\). Using mathematical induction, prove that \(c_n = 3^n - n\) for all positive integers \(n\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Note that L.H.S.} &= c_1 = 2 \\ \text{R.H.S.} &= 3^1 - 1 = 2 = \text{L.H.S.} \end{aligned} \]

Therefore, the statement is true for \(n=1\).

Assume that \(c_m = 3^m - m\), where \(m\) is a positive integer.

\[ \begin{aligned} c_{m+1} &= 3c_m + 2m - 1 \\ &= 3(3^m - m) + 2m - 1 \\ &= 3^{m+1} - 3m + 2m - 1 \\ &= 3^{m+1} - m - 1 \\ &= 3^{m+1} - (m+1) \end{aligned} \]

So, the statement is true for \(n = m+1\) if it is true for \(n = m\).

By mathematical induction, the statement is true for all positive integers \(n\).
