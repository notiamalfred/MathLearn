    Glossary of Key Terms

    Every definition from the notes, in one place.

    Discriminant (Δ)

    The expression Δ = b² − 4ac for a quadratic equation ax² + bx + c = 0.

    Completing Square

    Rewriting a quadratic expression ax² + bx + c in the form a(x − h)² + k.

    Sum of roots

    For ax² + bx + c = 0, α + β = −b/a.

    Product of roots

    For ax² + bx + c = 0, αβ = c/a.

    Complex number

    A number of the form a + bi, where a and b are real numbers and i = √(−1).

    Conjugate

    The conjugate of a + bi is a − bi.

    General form (quadratic)

    y = ax² + bx + c. Shows opening, axis of symmetry and y-intercept.

    Vertex form (quadratic)

    y = a(x − h)² + k. Shows vertex (h, k) and axis of symmetry x = h.

    Intercept form (quadratic)

    y = a(x − α)(x − β). Shows x-intercepts α and β.

    Axis of symmetry

    A vertical line that passes through the vertex of the parabola and lies exactly midway between the two x-intercepts (if they exist). Equation: x = −b/(2a).

    Centroid (G)

    The point where the three medians of a triangle meet. Always lies inside the triangle. Coordinates: ((x_a + x_b + x_c)/3, (y_a + y_b + y_c)/3). AG : GD = 2 : 1.

    Orthocenter (H)

    The point where the three altitudes of a triangle meet. Inside for acute, at the right-angle vertex for right-angled, outside for obtuse.

    Circumcenter (O)

    The center of the circumscribed circle, formed by the intersection of the perpendicular bisectors. OA = OB = OC (radii).

    Incenter (I)

    The center of the inscribed circle, formed by the intersection of the angle bisectors. DI = EI = FI (radii). pr = 2A. Coordinates: ((ax_a + bx_b + cx_c)/(a + b + c), (ay_a + by_b + cy_c)/(a + b + c)).

    Euler's line

    For all triangles, the circumcenter O, the centroid V and the orthocenter H lie on the same line.

    Perpendicular Bisector Property

    If PQ is a perpendicular bisector of BC, then any points on PQ can form isosceles sides with BC (e.g. PB = PC and OB = OC).

    Angle Bisector Property

    If BI is an angle bisector of ABC with AB ⊥ DI and BC ⊥ IF, then DI = IF.

    Angle Bisector Theorem

    BM/MC = AB/AC, similarly for other two pairs.

    i (imaginary unit)

    Definition: i = √(−1). Also i^(4n + r) = i^r for non-negative integers n and r.

    Compound Angle Formula

    Formulas that express trigonometric functions of sums or differences of angles in terms of functions of individual angles. e.g., \(\sin(A \pm B) = \sin A \cos B \pm \cos A \sin B\).

    Double Angle Formula

    Formulas that express trigonometric functions of twice an angle in terms of functions of the original angle. e.g., \(\sin 2A = 2\sin A\cos A\).

    Product-to-Sum Formula

    Identities that convert products of trigonometric functions into sums or differences, such as \(\sin A \cos B = \frac{1}{2}[\sin(A+B) + \sin(A-B)]\).

    Sum-to-Product Formula

    Identities that convert sums or differences of trigonometric functions into products, such as \(\sin A + \sin B = 2\sin\left(\frac{A+B}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{A-B}{2}\right)\).

    Range Adjustment (Trigonometric Equations)

    A method used when solving equations where the angle is manipulated (e.g., \(\sin 2x\)). The domain is adjusted by the same multiple to find all valid roots before solving for the base variable.

    Long Division (Rational Trig Functions)

    A method used to find the maximum and minimum values of rational trigonometric functions by separating the numerator by the denominator into the form \(m + \frac{n}{ct + d}\).

    Perfect Square Form (Trigonometric Functions)

    A form like \(y = (a \sin \theta + b)^2\) used to find max/min values by checking if 0 is within the range of the inner expression \(at + b\).

    Quadratic Form (Trigonometric Functions)

    A function like \(y = a \sin^2 \theta + b \sin \theta + c\). Maximum and minimum values are found by completing the square to find the vertex and checking the end-points \(t = \pm 1\).
