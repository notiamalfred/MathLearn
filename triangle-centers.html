Triangle Centers

Centroid, orthocenter, circumcenter, incenter — and their special features.

Median & Centroid Altitude & Orthocenter Perpendicular Bisector Angle Bisector & Incenter Other Features

Median and Centroid

Special Lines and Centers — Median and Centroid

AQ is the median of ΔABC. G is the centroid of ΔABC.

Position

Always lie inside the triangle.

Features

  1. AG : GD = BG : GE = CG : GF = 2 : 1
  2. The 6 smaller triangles have the same area
  3. The 3 middle triangles have the same area
  4. The 2 larger triangles have the same area
  5. The centroid of ΔABC is also the centroid of ΔDEF
  6. Coordinates of centroid: ((x_a + x_b + x_c)/3, (y_a + y_b + y_c)/3)

Altitude and Orthocenter

Special Lines and Centers — Altitude and Orthocenter

AK is the altitude of ΔABC. H is the orthocenter of ΔABC.

Position

  • Case 1: Acute-angled Triangle — Always lie inside the triangle
  • Case 2: Right-angled Triangle — At the vertex which forms the right angle
  • Case 3: Obtuse-angled Triangle — Always lie outside the triangle

Features

  1. For each smaller right-angled triangle exists 3 pairs of similar triangles, totally 9 pairs:

    ΔALH ~ ΔACK ~ ΔBKH ~ ΔBLC ΔAMH ~ ΔABK ~ ΔCKH ~ ΔCMB ΔBLH ~ ΔABM ~ ΔCMH ~ ΔCLA (For right-angled triangle ΔABC, only 3 pairs in total)
  2. 3 cyclic quadrilaterals are formed (ALHM, LHBK, HMCK)
  3. By considering the area of triangle, AK × BC = BM × AC = CL × AB (altitude × base always equal to each other)

  4. The orthocenter and vertex can inter-exchange.

    e.g. the orthocenter of ΔABC is H, while

    • The orthocenter of ΔABH is C
    • The orthocenter of ΔACH is B
    • The orthocenter of ΔBCH is A

Coordinates of Orthocenter

Can be found by joining simultaneous equations of two altitudes. Keep an eye on horizontal lines and vertical lines.

Perpendicular Bisector and Circumcenter

Special Lines and Centers — Perpendicular Bisector and Circumcenter

PQ is the perpendicular bisector of ΔABC. O is the circumcenter of ΔABC.

In right-angled triangle, BROQ is a rectangle. As well as the perpendicular bisector of AC does not pass through B unless AB = BC.

Features

  1. Perpendicular Bisector Property + Converse of Perpendicular Bisector Property
  2. A circumscribed circle is formed, passing through points A, B and C with center O. The circle passing through obtuse-angled triangle is the largest given same perimeter.
  3. OA = OB = OC (radii). All the vertices are equidistant from the circumcenter. Note that in scalene triangle, OQ, OR and OS are unequal!
  4. 3 pairs of congruent triangles are formed: ΔARO and ΔBRO; ΔASO and ΔCSO; ΔBQO and ΔCQO
  5. The circumcenter of ΔABC, O is also the orthocenter of ΔRSQ.

Perpendicular Bisector Property

If PQ is a perpendicular bisector of BC, then any points on PQ can form isosceles sides with BC. e.g. PB = PC and OB = OC.

Converse of Perpendicular Bisector Property

  1. OB = OC and BQ = QC → OQ ⊥ BC
  2. OB = OC and OQ ⊥ BC → BQ = QC

Coordinates of Circumcenter

Can be found by:

  1. Setting equations on equal circumradii (more preferred)
  2. Joining simultaneous equations of two perpendiculars

Keep an eye on horizontal lines and vertical lines.

Angle Bisector and Incenter

Special Lines and Centers — Angle Bisector and Incenter

AM is the angle bisector of ΔABC. I is the incenter of ΔABC.

Features

  1. Angle Bisector Property + Converse of Angle Bisector Property
  2. An inscribed circle is formed, passing through points D, E and F with center I. The circle passing through acute-angled triangle is the largest given same perimeter.
  3. DI = EI = FI (radii), all the perpendiculars are equidistant from the incenter. Note that in scalene triangle, AI, BI and CI are unequal.
  4. pr = 2A
  5. 3 pairs of congruent triangles are formed: ΔADI and ΔAEI; ΔBDI and ΔBFI; ΔCEI and ΔCFI
  6. From (5): AD = AE, BD = BF, CE = CF
  7. Angle Bisector Theorem: BM/MC = AB/AC, similarly for other two pairs
  8. Coordinates of incenter: ((ax_a + bx_b + cx_c)/(a + b + c), (ay_a + by_b + cy_c)/(a + b + c))

Angle Bisector Property

If BI is an angle bisector of ABC with AB ⊥ DI and BC ⊥ IF, then DI = IF.

Converse of Angle Bisector Property

If DI = IF with AB ⊥ DI and BC ⊥ IF, then BI is an angle bisector of ABC. Please note that I can be any points lying on the angle bisector.

Coordinates of Incenter

Can be found by:

  1. Make use of pr = 2A
  2. Setting up equations related to inclinations
  3. Joining simultaneous equations of two angle bisectors when there are horizontal/vertical lines

Other Features

Euler's line

For all triangles, the circumcenter O, the centroid V and the orthocenter H lie on the same line which is known as the Euler's line.

Isosceles triangle

For isosceles triangles, the angle bisector of BAC (AQ) acts as all 4 special lines and hence all 4 centers lie on AQ (the axis of symmetry).

(Given that AB = AC, O, G, I, H are the circumcenter, centroid, incenter, orthocenter respectively)

Equilateral triangle

For equilateral triangles, the 4 centers coincide at the same point.

(G is the circumcenter, centroid, incenter, orthocenter)
